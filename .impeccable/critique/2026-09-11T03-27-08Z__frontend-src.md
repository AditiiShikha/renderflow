## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: This is a partially-authored industrial HMI skin over a fundamentally generic "AI generates a screen" shell. The non-transferable details are real: the blueprint "+" corner registration marks (`Corners.jsx`, applied to ~10 element types), the live threshold tick marks drawn directly on `Gauge.jsx`'s bar, the status-tinted pump/conveyor schematics, and copy like "LOCAL MACHINE CONTEXT CONNECTED" / "LLM · Phi-3 · On-Prem." Those could not be pasted into a generic SaaS product unchanged.

But strip the palette and corner marks and the interaction skeleton underneath is stock LLM-chat-app chrome: a centered prompt input with "Try:" suggestion chips, a phased "thinking" checklist overlay, a right-hand history drawer, and a dismissible top-right toast for events. That's Perplexity/Copilot chrome, not an operator console — whose mental model is "the plant pushes state at me and I acknowledge/drill down," not "I type a request and wait for a stream." The clearest symptom: the product's most safety-critical event (a critical alarm) uses the *identical visual grammar* as an AI assistant surfacing a suggested follow-up.

## Overall Impression

The bones of a genuinely distinctive industrial HMI are here — the blueprint corner marks, the gauge threshold ticks, and the status-tinted schematics are real authored details, not template filling. But the product hasn't yet earned its own premise. Motion currently communicates *color* (via CSS variables) but not *causality or magnitude* — a pump spins at the same 2.6s rate whether it's idle or in critical alarm. And the single moment where "believable control room" matters most — a live critical alarm — is dressed as a dismissible SaaS toast that auto-navigates the operator away after 2.6 seconds with no consent step. The single biggest opportunity is making the alarm pathway and the schematic motion actually carry the state information the visual language implies they carry.

## What's Working

1. **Gauge threshold ticks** (`Gauge.jsx`'s `warnPct`/`critPct` marks drawn on the bar) — mirrors how a real analog/digital gauge face communicates danger zones from the backend's actual threshold values. Domain thinking, not a generic progress bar.
2. **Blueprint corner-mark system** (`Corners.jsx` + `.rf-corner` in `index.css`) — cheap, consistent, and the one visual signature that would look wrong if pasted into an unrelated dashboard.
3. **Fail-safe spec rendering** (`ScreenCanvas.jsx`'s `UnknownWidget`, the "Unknown tag" branches in `Gauge`/`Trend`/`NumericCard`) — a visible, labeled degradation instead of a crash or silent drop, which is the right posture for a UI whose input is an LLM-generated spec it can't fully trust.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Alarm semantics are incoherent and reuse the product's only danger signal for routine events**
*Why it matters*: In a product whose premise is "believable control room," the alarm pathway is the one interaction that must be unambiguous. Right now: `EventNotification` auto-navigates the operator away 2600ms after an alarm fires with no consent (`App.jsx`); dismissing that toast doesn't acknowledge anything in `AlarmBanner.jsx` — two disconnected ack models exist; `useAlarms`'s `online` flag is silently dropped rather than surfaced; and `rf-alarmglow` (red danger glow) is reused verbatim on brand-new *non-alarm* `HistorySidebar` entries. Together these teach an operator to distrust red, which is the opposite of the product's goal.
*Fix*: Remove the auto-navigate timer (require an explicit click to view); unify both ack surfaces around one alarm-state model; surface alarm-poll health as a second offline banner alongside the existing telemetry one; reserve `rf-alarmglow` exclusively for genuine critical state and give new-history-item feedback its own calmer treatment.
*Suggested command*: `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] The declared "technical premium" typography isn't real — every heading silently falls back to system fonts**
*Why it matters*: Typography is explicitly load-bearing for the "premium industrial" feel the brief calls for. Confirmed by direct evidence: no Barlow/Barlow Condensed font files exist anywhere in the repo, and `index.css` documents this was a deliberate removal for the air-gapped requirement with no replacement bundled. A condensed technical display face vs. default Segoe UI/Arial is the difference between "purpose-built instrument panel" and "default OS dialog," and it's happening silently on every install.
*Fix*: Self-host actual Barlow/Barlow Condensed `.woff2` files (consistent with the air-gapped constraint already driving other decisions here), or deliberately choose a genuinely bundled condensed/technical face and update the stack intentionally.
*Suggested command*: `$impeccable typeset`

**[P1] Motion communicates color/state but never causality or magnitude**
*Why it matters*: This is the clearest gap between the brief ("motion that reflects real machine/telemetry state") and the code. `rf-rotate` and the SVG `animateMotion` flow dots run at fixed durations (2.6s/2.2s) regardless of any real speed/flow/vibration tag — a pump idling and a pump in critical alarm animate identically except for a color swap. Warning and critical states also share identical `rf-alarmglow`/`rf-pulse` timing, differing only in color. `rf-pulse` itself is overloaded: the same "breathing" language is used for a neutral mic-listening icon, a critical alarm bell, and a routine trend-line dot that fires on every data point regardless of status — diluting what "pulse" is supposed to mean.
*Fix*: Drive rotation/flow speed off a real tag where available (or a normalized activity value); scale animation frequency/amplitude with severity, not just color; let idle/stopped assets visibly stop moving; reserve `rf-pulse` for genuine attention-worthy moments.
*Suggested command*: `$impeccable animate`

**[P2] The alarm notification reads as a SaaS toast, not a plant annunciator**
*Why it matters*: `EventNotification.jsx` — fixed, corner-anchored, `shadow-lg`, 300px, small X to dismiss — is visually indistinguishable from a marketing-site "new feature" popup. This directly undermines "believable control room" at the exact moment it matters most.
*Fix*: Redesign as a persistent, docked annunciator strip that must be explicitly acknowledged/cleared, matching real SCADA/HMI alarm-banner convention, instead of an auto-timed dismissible card.
*Suggested command*: `$impeccable polish`

**[P3] Accessibility and edge-case gaps are systemic, not isolated**
*Why it matters*: Confirmed by direct counts — only 5 of 10 buttons carry an `aria-label` (and the ones that exist, like `Toggle.jsx`'s literal `"Toggle"`, aren't distinguishing between multiple toggles on the same screen); zero `role=`/`tabIndex` attributes anywhere; zero `prefers-reduced-motion` handling despite 5 of 6 keyframe animations looping infinitely; no visible custom `:focus-visible` styling anywhere; `Gauge.jsx`'s percentage math has no null guard before its `Math.max/min` clamp, so a `null` telemetry value produces `NaN`-driven inline styles instead of a designed "no data" state; `ScreenCanvas.jsx` has no empty-state for a zero-widget spec. These are exactly the conditions (screen readers, keyboard-only operation, degraded backend data) an industrial tool is more likely to hit than a typical SaaS app.
*Fix*: Add a `prefers-reduced-motion` override block in `index.css`; give each `Toggle`/disclosure real, distinct ARIA labels and `aria-expanded` state; add visible focus-ring styling in the brand's palette; null-guard `Gauge`'s math and give `ScreenCanvas` an explicit empty state.
*Suggested command*: `$impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User / shift operator)**: No keyboard shortcuts anywhere — even acknowledging an alarm requires a mouse click. History is capped at 8 entries in `localStorage` with no search or pin, and is device-local rather than shared across control-room workstations, making it useless as a shift log. The hardcoded 2.6s auto-navigation on any critical alarm can yank Alex off whatever screen he's actively working from without consent — precisely the kind of "unnecessary interruption" that drives power users to distrust a tool.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**: `Toggle.jsx` gives every switch the identical `aria-label="Toggle"` — indistinguishable to a screen reader when multiple toggles appear on one screen. `WidgetCard.jsx`'s "why" disclosure has no `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`, so a screen reader gets no signal that content appeared. Severity is conveyed almost entirely by color plus 10-11px text at 50-70% opacity (timestamps, header subtitle, "Try:" label) — a real contrast-risk cluster on an already-dark surface, with zero `prefers-reduced-motion` support to fall back on either.

**Riley (Stress Tester)**: `useAlarms`'s `online` flag is fetched but never read — a dead alarm poll produces zero UI signal while telemetry keeps updating normally, making the failure invisible. A spec with an empty `widgets` array renders a bare title with no "nothing to show" messaging. `Gauge.jsx` computes bar width straight from `value`/`min`/`max` with no null guard, so a `null` telemetry reading produces `NaN`-driven inline styles rather than a designed "no data" bar.

## Minor Observations

- `BootScreen.jsx`'s splash runs a fixed 1500ms regardless of whether `contextLoading` actually finished — on a slow backend it states "Online" before that's confirmed; on a fast one it runs longer than needed. Tie the duration to the real loading state.
- The same flame/bar-chart glyph is reused as the brand mark in `BootScreen`, `Header`, and `WelcomeState` — consistent, but reads as a generic "analytics" icon rather than anything specific to pumps/conveyors/packaging.
- `PromptBar.jsx`'s Generate button uses `hover:-translate-y-px active:scale-[0.97]` — a marketing-site button micro-interaction that feels distinctly un-industrial next to the otherwise flat, hairline-bordered aesthetic elsewhere.
- `DiagnosticTable.jsx` renders unknown-tag rows inline with real ones, differentiated only by an "UNKNOWN" status badge — easy to miss at a glance in a dense table.
- 17 raw hex colors (`#46c17d`, `#e0554a`, etc.) are hardcoded across 9 files instead of referencing the existing `var(--color-*)` tokens already used everywhere else — a token-hygiene gap the deterministic scan surfaced that pure code reading would likely have missed.

## Questions to Consider

- If the goal is a control-room HMI rather than an AI copilot, why does the primary interaction start with a text prompt and three suggestion chips — a pattern users already associate with ChatGPT/Copilot — instead of something mapped to how an operator actually thinks (select an asset, drill into an alarm, choose from known screens)?
- The product's one safety-critical moment — a live critical alarm — currently shares its visual grammar with "you just added something to your history." Has the design actually earned an operator's trust that red means "stop and look now," or does it currently mean "something changed, glance if you feel like it"?
- Every schematic spins and every flow-dot travels constantly, at the same rate, whether the asset is idle or in critical alarm. If motion doesn't carry information beyond color, what does constant animation buy — and would a screen that's calm by default and only moves when something changes actually feel more like a premium instrument?
